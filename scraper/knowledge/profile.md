# Company profile and scoring guide

This file is public. It describes what the company does and how to judge fit.
Private details (turnover, contract sizes, empanelments, named clients, bid
history) come from the COMPANY_KNOWLEDGE secret and are added after this text.

## Who we are

A large Indian communications and PR agency with a dedicated Government
Practice. Headquartered in Delhi with an in-house production and creative
studio, offices in Mumbai, Bengaluru and Chennai, and working seats in many
state capitals and tier 2 cities. Content teams write in English and Hindi.

## What we bid for (strong fit)

- Social media management and content for central ministries, PSUs and
  statutory bodies: retainers, agencies of record, empanelments.
- Social media monitoring, analysis and online reputation management,
  including a 24x7 rostered desk with a licensed listening tool.
- 360 degree PR and communications agency roles, media relations.
- Digital media outreach and campaigns, including bulk SMS, OBD and social
  components priced against DAVP/CBC rates.
- Creative and video production: reels, documentaries, explainers, motion
  graphics, roundup videos, AI enabled content at scale.
- Government communications retainers generally (IEC/BCC where the work is
  content, creative and digital rather than physical supply).

## Partial fit

- Websites and microsites delivered as content and SEO led builds.
- Influencer programmes.
- Event and summit communications, case studies, decks, reports.
- Public affairs, advocacy, CSR, corporate, executive, internal and crisis
  communications.

## Weak or no fit

- Training of trainers, curriculum and course module development (consortium
  only).
- Software, app and platform engineering, web hosting, technical management.
- Paid media buying as the main scope.
- Goods, construction, equipment supply, printing and physical outdoor media.
- Tenders that make an empanelment mandatory that the company does not hold
  (see the private notes; CBC AV Producer empanelment is not held).

## Who we bid for

- Highest: central ministries and their bodies, PSUs, regulators and central
  banks, especially information and broadcasting, external affairs, ports and
  shipping, highways, railways, civil aviation, culture, tribal affairs,
  sports, trade and investment promotion, health.
- Medium: state development authorities, state missions, development and
  non-profit bodies, industry associations.
- Low: private sector tenders (outside the usual pursuit pattern).
- State, district and city bodies are a normal market for the same services:
  a core scope for them is still a strong fit.
- Most work is bought on GeM under QCBS.

## Eligibility checks

Fit says whether the work suits the company; eligibility says whether it can
qualify. Check both, and keep everything the private notes say about
eligibility (turnover, MSME/Udyam classification and EMD exemption,
empanelments held and not held, offices, team size, past work orders).

- Value: compare the estimated value with the usual contract range and the
  turnover in the private notes. Far above the range needs a deliberate check.
- EMD: the company is MSME/Udyam registered, and Udyam has been accepted for
  EMD exemption. Note when an EMD is stated and whether the exemption likely
  applies; check the tender's MSE clause against the right classification year.
- Empanelment: if the title demands an empanelment or registration the company
  does not hold, it cannot qualify: Skip. If it isn't recorded either way
  (for example DAVP), say to confirm it.
- Experience: similar work for central government, PSUs or statutory bodies
  is the company's strongest evidence; sector specific experience criteria
  (sports, health, monitoring tools) are where bids have been lost.
- Presence: tenders needing state or city offices can use the multi-city
  working seats.
- Language: content teams write English and Hindi. If the tender needs
  another Indian language, say to confirm it can be staffed (for example
  with freelancers or a partner). This is an eligibility check, not a reason
  to lower the fit score.
- Always end by naming what to confirm in the tender document.

The dashboard is public: describe eligibility in general terms and never quote
private figures, never name the company's clients, prospects or bids, and never
describe a relationship with the tender's buyer.

## How to score

The score is about the work: does the scope match what the company does?

- Scope match sets the score. Core services above: 8 to 10. Partial fit
  list: 5 to 7. Only a small part fits: 3 to 4. Weak or no fit list: 1 to 2.
- Client fit only nudges borderline scores (5 to 7) by up to 1. It never
  pulls a core scope below 7. An unnamed buyer is not a penalty.
- Mandatory empanelment the company does not hold: Skip, and say which one.
- These never lower the score or the recommendation; mention them in the
  reason or eligibility instead: a close deadline, a missing buyer, value or
  EMD, a regional language, a small or very large value, sectional cut-offs.
- If the tender looks like one in the private bid pipeline, say only "check
  the internal bid tracker".
